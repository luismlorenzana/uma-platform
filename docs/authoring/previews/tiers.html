The card names the valkyrie or construct, and the search finds her other battlesuits. Any imported unit's card has Leave out of every board (P121): it goes to its board's Left out row and is on no board's list, so it never flows to Key or Regular. Playstyles: on the card, not the tier list

The owner moved away from the class split (P123, retiring P117's grid). Both Uma boards are plain tier rows now, like every other board, and the home's playstyle table is gone. A character's playstyle is still her lead outfit's (her best old tier, then her original outfit, then the lowest id), or the pick on her card when her outfits span two. It shows on her card with the outfit's four grades, the Every playstyle filter narrows the board to one, and the plan keeps it. It never places a portrait.

Playable Umas starts over on the six-tier scale. The earlier ranks (20 SS, 20 S, 20 A, 33 B, 42 F) are kept in tiers/archive/umas.r49.2026-11-10.json, and Import board brings them back in one undoable step. Uma NPCs keeps its ranks; until Playable is ranked again, every Uma is on it.

How leftovers flow

A board never changes another. It only decides who stays in its cast; whoever it leaves out appears on the board below, with a chip saying where they came from.

Careers: at least three bosses each

The owner wants every Uma to have a career, a story mode where she fights her way through at least three bosses, and more where there are bosses to spare. The recommended rule (P116): a career draws its bosses from its own tier first. A tier with more than three bosses per Uma lengthens its careers to ceil(bosses ÷ Umas); one with fewer shares its bosses. The Studio's home computes this live.

Why local only

  1. It's taste, not data. Who is playable, who is a boss and who comes first change often; a fast local tool fits them, a release schema doesn't.
  2. The references are private. Every portrait is original franchise art. Like the Uma sprites, it never goes in git, uma-art or a public URL.
  3. Nothing ships from here. The boards decide what gets authored and when; the art still passes its own gates and releases.
  4. No cloud, no cost. Loopback only, atomic JSON files, and the existing private backup.
  5. One place to look. Two rankers on two scales became eleven boards on one, with one home.
